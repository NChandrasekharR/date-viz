Dating & Data — Part II

The Road to a Date Has Three Gates

Following all 5,601 matches through reply, invitation, and meeting — and what the 18 that made it all the way did differently.

← Back to the main story

Every date flowed through an invite

Of the 207 conversations, those where someone floated a concrete plan — a day, a time, a place — met at 16%. A vague “we should hang out sometime” managed 7%. Conversations where nobody asked at all: 1 date in 66.

Depth is destiny

Meeting odds climb with every extra stretch of conversation — from 1.6% for chats that died within five messages to 26% for the ones that crossed forty. The median conversation that became a date ran 59 messages; the median one that didn’t ran 10.

Dates are marathons, not sprints

The typical conversation that led to a date stayed alive for 45 hours; the typical one that didn’t lasted 41 minutes. On the log–log plane the dates cluster in one corner: long and wordy.

Chats born on Monday go places

Conversations that started early in the week converted best — of Monday starters became dates. Friday starters: none. Small samples, wide error bars, but the weekend slump is consistent.

Method notes: “Date” = conversations marked “Yes” in the export’s met survey field; unanswered surveys count as no date, matching the funnel stats. Invite detection is keyword-based on visible messages. Lifespan is first-to-last message; 27 single-burst chats with zero measured span are drawn at the scatter’s left edge. Rates are descriptive, not causal — deep conversations may cause dates, or dates-in-the-making may cause deep conversations.
