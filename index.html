Dating & Data · A forensic report

5,601 People Said Yes.
18 Dates Happened.

Six years of Hinge, exported and interrogated. What follows is everything the data would admit to — the silence, the sliver, the asks that worked, and the conversations that refused to die.

Act I

The Void

Every bar chart lies a little. This one doesn't. Below: all 5,601 matches, drawn to scale. The gray is people who matched and then said nothing, ever. The blue stripe is everyone who replied. The dates are in there too. You may need to squint.

96.3% of matching is mutual agreement to never speak. The rest of this page lives inside that blue stripe. Let's zoom in.

Act II

Inside the Sliver, Every Date Flowed Through an Ask

The 207 conversations sort themselves by one question: did anyone actually propose a plan? A concrete ask — a day, a time, a place — converted at 16%. A vague “we should hang out sometime” managed 7%, which is what “sometime” deserves. And when nobody asked at all: 1 date in 66, presumably by accident.

Here's the surprise: asking early doesn't matter. Asks in the first five messages and asks after message five convert identically. Timing is a myth. Commitment isn't — say Thursday, not sometime.

Act III

Most Conversations Die Before Dinner

Conversations on Hinge don't end. They just… stop. Half of all conversations were over — last message sent, forever — within — hours of starting. Only —% were still alive a day later, and —% made it past two days. But the ones that crossed 72 hours became dates at —× the rate of the ones that didn't.

If a conversation is still breathing on day three, it isn't small talk anymore. It's a plan forming.

Act IV

Depth Is Destiny

Every extra stretch of conversation roughly triples the odds. Chats that died within five messages became dates 1.6% of the time. Cross forty messages and it's 26% — one in four. The median conversation that became a date ran 59 messages. The median one that didn't ran 10.

A caution from the methods desk: message count doesn't cause dates — mutual interest causes both. Nobody ever typed their way out of indifference.

Act V

The Data Reviewed Your Texting Style. It Shrugged.

The working theory was that curiosity wins — ask more questions, keep messages tight, be charming. The data disagrees, gently. The conversations that became dates asked slightly fewer questions and wrote slightly shorter messages than the ones that went nowhere. Nearly identical, honestly. Chemistry, it turns out, does not read your drafts.

Act VI

Dates Are Marathons, Not Sprints

The typical conversation that led to a date stayed alive for 45 hours; the typical one that didn't lasted 41 minutes. And here's the tender part: in the date conversations, the first invitation showed up early — and then a median of 57 more messages followed it. The ask wasn't a closing move. It was a doorway, and both people kept talking on the other side of it.

Act VII

Chats Born on Monday Go Places

Conversations that started early in the week converted best — — of Monday starters became dates. Friday starters went zero for thirty-two, a result so clean it feels personal. Small samples, wide error bars — but apparently romance is a weekday project.

Zoom all the way out and six years of activity looks like weather: long droughts, two spring storms, and one very determined 2025.

Act VIII

The Opener Autopsy

Does the cheesy pickup line work? Does “hey” doom you? This section measures opener style — cheese markers, low-effort greetings, length, emoji — against what actually happened next.

The whole story, one number at a time

5,601 → 207 → 141 → 18

Matched, replied, someone asked, and eighteen times a person from a screen became a person across a table.

The data can't tell you which conversations will make it. It can only tell you what the survivors had in common: somebody proposed something real, and then — this is the whole secret — neither person let it die.

Methods & caveats: “Date” = conversations marked “Yes” in the export's met survey field; unanswered surveys count as no date, so every rate here is a floor. Invite detection is keyword-based on visible messages (the export contains only one side of each chat). Lifespan is first-to-last message; single-burst chats with zero measured span sit at the scatter's left edge. Confidence intervals are binomial Wilson; survival is Kaplan–Meier with censoring past one week. All of it is descriptive, not causal — deep conversations may cause dates, or dates-in-the-making may cause deep conversations. With 18 positive events, treat every sub-split as a good story, not a law of nature.

No chat content, names, or identifiers are stored in this site's data — only counts, durations, and rates.
